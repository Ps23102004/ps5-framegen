## 1. Verdict on the starting design

**Build the capture-only player first.** My proposed frame-generation schedule adds **33.33 ms over a matched capture-only path** on a fixed 120 Hz display, provided the midpoint meets its deadline. That leaves **26.67 ms for the entire capture-only path** to meet the 60 ms TV-relative goal. The other deciding measurement is whether the display can sustain a refresh rate matched to the source. An exact 120 Hz display and a 59.94 Hz source require periodic corrections.

Throughout this plan, **M** means measured, with its source identified. **E** means estimate or calculation. Proposed thresholds, queue capacities, and illustrative timings are engineering estimates, not measurements. **Target** denotes a number supplied in the request. Nominal hardware and signal specifications are inputs to the estimates.

| Starting choice | Verdict and reason |
|---|---|
| Capture | **Keep, conditionally.** Select a native uncompressed SDR device mode and inspect delivered buffers. Requesting `420v` at the app output alone does not establish what the card sent. Inspect `AVCaptureDevice.formats`, `activeFormat`, `AVCaptureDeviceFormat.formatDescription`, and `videoSupportedFrameRateRanges` in `AVCaptureDevice.h`. `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput.videoSettings` in `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput.h` documents an empty dictionary for native output. |
| Cadence | **Change.** Lock to observed duplicate/change transitions. On clean input, confirm a suspected duplicate against the complete active image. Launch on the first changed copy once locked. Section 4 specifies the transition behavior. |
| Interpolation | **Keep.** Use temporal-only interpolation and one midpoint. **M:** `1920×1080`, `420v`, and a successful midpoint run. The temporal-only initializer and the previous/current/phase contract are in `VTFrameProcessor_LowLatencyFrameInterpolation.h`, `VTLowLatencyFrameInterpolationConfiguration.initWithFrameWidth:frameHeight:numberOfInterpolatedFrames:` and `VTLowLatencyFrameInterpolationParameters.initWithSourceFrame:previousFrame:interpolationPhase:destinationFrames:`. [Probe](~/Developer/ps5-framegen/probes/interp_speed.m) · [Run](~/Developer/ps5-framegen/probes/out/run-10min-during-render.txt) |
| Upscale | **Change the first implementation.** Use one shader for video-range Y′CbCr conversion and bilinear scaling on both paths. **E:** `3456/1920 = 1944/1080 = 1.8`, with `(2234−1944)/2 = 145` black pixels above and below. This gives capture-only and generated video the same measurable render path. Compare MetalFX spatial scaling after the latency gate. |
| Presentation | **Change.** Schedule content by source PTS on observed display slots. Request a source-matched display rate first. Use the fixed-refresh algorithm in section 5 when the display actually runs at 120 Hz. |
| Audio | **Keep.** Schedule captured game audio against the same host-time mapping as video, with a measured offset for the audio output device. Include mode transitions in the sync test. |
| HUD | **Keep.** Show capture sample rate, unique-frame rate, actual presented-content intervals, cadence state, intentional clock corrections, missed midpoints, capture drops, and estimated latency. Label the camera-measured TV offset separately. |
| Concurrent OBS | **Gate early.** With OBS already streaming in its existing configuration, open the player, then verify that both continue receiving advancing video and audio. A successful device open settles only the first half of that test. |
| Plan B | **Defer.** Optical flow plus warp and a two-frame RIFE-class model retain the future-frame dependency. Benchmark either only if the capture/display path passes and VT image quality or compute cost is the remaining failure. |

## 2. Latency budget

Let **E** `H = 1001/60000 s = 16.683 ms` be one HDMI period, `U = 2H = 33.367 ms` one unique-frame period, and `S = 1000/120 = 8.333 ms` one nominal display slot. Set time zero at the first copy of real frame **A** reaching the app. Under regular capture delivery, the first copy of **B** arrives at `2H`. The midpoint **M** represents source time `H`.

**E:** With `13 ms` compute, M first exists at `2H + 13 = 46.37 ms` after A. Its lateness relative to **its own** source time is `46.37 − H = 29.68 ms`. To display `A, M, B` evenly, the earliest corresponding continuous-time schedule is approximately `29.68, 46.37, 63.05 ms`. Presenting A immediately would leave a `46.37 ms` first interval. Waiting for B’s *second* copy before dispatch would add another `16.68 ms`.

The table budgets the midpoint from its represented source time, assuming a common capture delay. `J` is late arrival of B relative to that model, `K` is detection, dispatch and any required input copy, and `R` is the path from VT completion through scaling to eligibility for a display slot. Positive timing allowances below are incremental unless identified as common.

**Is the 35 ms target reachable? Yes, conditionally.** The source-relative subtotal and the capture-only differential are different measurements. Use the same renderer and earliest eligible slot calculation in both modes. Delay the real frame by exactly four display slots in generation mode. Apply the same offset to the midpoint’s nominal slot. The slot rounding then cancels for matched real frames. **E:** `4S = 33.333 ms`, leaving `35−33.333 = 1.667 ms` over the scheduled differential. A one-slot miss makes it `5S = 41.667 ms`. Score misses explicitly.

In the least favorable clock phase the future endpoint leaves **E** `4S−H = 16.650 ms` for compute plus all costs beyond the matched capture/render allowance. At **M** `12.80 ms` median compute that leaves **E** `3.850 ms`. At **M** `14.32 ms` p99 it leaves **E** `2.330 ms`. At the idle **target** of `12 ms` it leaves **E** `4.650 ms`. This requires immediate first-copy dispatch, bounded capture jitter, the same render/latch behavior as the baseline, and no additional queued drawable or capture frame. Verify that the modeled baseline is the **fastest measured** capture-only path. Artificially delaying the control makes the subtraction meaningless.

For the overall **target**, **E:** `60−33.33 = 26.67 ms` maximum capture-only lag with this schedule. A baseline at the separate `50 ms` **target** would produce approximately `83.33 ms` overall. Treat that as a project decision before investing in a better interpolator.

## 4. Cadence detection algorithm

**Clean-input rule.** Start with exact equality as the duplicate threshold. An uncompressed format alone does not prove the card reproduces duplicates byte-for-byte. The first live test decides whether this rule applies.

- **Metric.** **E design:** sample one luma pixel per `8×8` block. At `1920×1080`, that is `240×135 = 32,400` samples. Compute the count of nonzero absolute differences and mean absolute difference versus the immediately preceding HDMI sample. Any changed sample proves the images differ. If the sample count is zero, compare all active Y and UV bytes, row by row, excluding stride padding. This catches small HUD-only and chroma-only updates. A duplicate `D` requires exactly zero active-byte differences. A changed image is `U`. For CPU plane access, `CVPixelBuffer.h` declares `CVPixelBufferLockBaseAddress`, `CVPixelBufferGetBaseAddressOfPlane`, `CVPixelBufferGetBytesPerRowOfPlane`, and `CVPixelBufferUnlockBaseAddress`.
- **Time gate.** **E design:** accept adjacent PTS differences in `[0.75H, 1.25H]`, approximately `[12.51, 20.85] ms` at nominal input. Require the two endpoint first-copy timestamps to differ by `2H ± 0.25H`. Use PTS for this test and separately record callback-arrival jitter. A drop notification, nonmonotonic PTS, or out-of-window interval resets the state.
- **Acquire.** Show the first sample immediately in pass-through. Track the start PTS and repeat count of the current identical-image run. A run of exactly two equal samples followed by a changed image gives `A,A,B`. If the time gates pass, this is a **provisional pair**. Start computing `M(A,B)` on that first B. The following sample `B` confirms phase. No future repeat is required to dispatch. On cold entry, move to the delayed presentation timeline at a real-frame boundary and hold the last image as needed. Expire any provisional midpoint whose slot has already passed.
- **Locked.** After the first copy of B, expect `D`; after that duplicate, expect `U`. On the expected `U`, close the pair, dispatch immediately, and schedule `real, midpoint, real`. On the expected `D`, update phase without submitting work. **E:** steady-state decision delay is zero extra HDMI samples. A contradictory observable transition is acted on in its own callback.
- **Breaks.** An unexpected `U` on a repeat slot means a consecutive changed frame, as in a native-rate menu. An unexpected `D` on a change slot means a held image, stall, or static scene. A `2H` PTS gap can mean a lost HDMI sample. For all three, invalidate pending synthetic frames, retain the latest real image, and return to pass-through on the next available slot. Skip stale queued content when reducing latency. For irregular cadence, including a possible 40 fps mode, stay in pass-through until a fresh timed `A,A,B` candidate appears. A run longer than two identical frames cannot serve as the first endpoint run for reacquisition.
- **Cuts.** **E starting heuristic:** reject interpolation across a pair if more than half the sampled pixels change by at least `32` luma codes. Show the real cut and reacquire. Compare against flash and fast-pan footage before keeping that threshold.
- **OBS replay.** Keep a separate diagnostic profile. Mask the measured webcam rectangle, exclude block boundaries around it, and label repeated/unique transitions by inspecting the gameplay region. Fit two nonoverlapping thresholds to the labeled luma-difference distributions, with the overlap treated as uncertain/pass-through. If the distributions overlap throughout, use that recording for VT speed and visual tests only. The existing probe simply keeps every second decoded frame. [Probe](~/Developer/ps5-framegen/probes/interp_speed.m)

**Decision limit.** `A,A,B` is compatible with both `A,A,B,B` and `A,A,B,C`. The first C is the earliest evidence of that break, **E:** one HDMI period after the first B. Revoke a pending midpoint if it is still uncommitted. An already displayed midpoint cannot be withdrawn. Likewise, a static picture contains no visible evidence of the game’s internal cadence. The one-frame bound applies to **observable contradictions**, not to proving a hidden mode change.

## 5. Pacing algorithm

1. **Put input and display on a common clock.** Read sample PTS, convert from the capture session clock to host time, and record callback arrival separately. `CMSampleBuffer.h` declares `CMSampleBufferGetPresentationTimeStamp`. `AVCaptureSession.h` documents that output sample timestamps are on `AVCaptureSession.synchronizationClock`. `CMSync.h` declares `CMSyncConvertTime` and `CMClockGetHostTimeClock`. Reset the mapping after a discontinuity or unavailable clock.
2. **Measure the actual slot grid.** Use `CAMetalDisplayLinkUpdate.targetPresentationTimestamp` to predict slots and its `drawable` for rendering. Set `CAMetalDisplayLink.preferredFrameRateRange` to request a rate twice the measured HDMI rate. These symbols and `metalDisplayLink:needsUpdate:` are in `CAMetalDisplayLink.h`. A preference is an experiment. Verify actual intervals before choosing the scheduling mode. **E design:** estimate slot duration from the median of the last `120` valid single-refresh deltas, about one second of evidence. Re-anchor slot phase from each update; do not integrate a rounded `8.33 ms` constant.
3. **Assign each content timestamp a slot.** For a real frame use its first-copy PTS. For a midpoint use `(PTS_A + PTS_B)/2`. Let `t(p)` be its converted host time plus the calibrated common capture/render allowance from the fast baseline, `g` the observed grid origin, and `S` the observed slot period. The proposed generation slot is `k(p) = ceil((t(p)−g)/S) + 4`. The baseline uses the same expression without `+4`. In implementation, retain fractional time or rational ticks between updates. On entry, commit only future slots in increasing content order. Submit against the assigned slot with the measured render lead. `MTLCommandBuffer.h` declares `presentDrawable:atTime:` for a host-time presentation. `MTLDrawable.h` declares `addPresentedHandler:` and `presentedTime`, including a zero value for an unpresented or skipped drawable.
4. **Use the slot difference as the drift controller.** On a source-matched panel, **E:** `2 × (60000/1001) = 119.8801 Hz`. Each output frame lasts two refreshes, or `16.683 ms`. The four-slot offset is then `33.367 ms`. On a fixed `120.000 Hz` panel, **E:** `120/(60000/1001) = 2.002` display slots per output. The ceiling calculation naturally holds most images for two slots and one image for three slots per approximately `1/0.002 = 500` output intervals. That long interval is `25.0 ms`, about once per `500H = 8.34 s`. Real and generated content remain ordered. A free-running two-slot timer would instead gain a full output frame in about `16.68 s`.
5. **Score the tradeoff honestly.** **E:** In the target ten minutes there are approximately `600/H = 35,964` output intervals when `H` is expressed in seconds, and about `72` single-slot corrections. Approximately `99.8%` of intervals remain inside the target window before any processing misses. The percentage passes `99.5%`; one correction every `8.34 s` fails the independent limit of one glitch per `15 s` if every out-of-window interval counts. I would count it. Select source-matched operation for acceptance if the panel sustains it. Otherwise report the fixed-grid result as a partial pass. Grouping two corrections into a four-slot hold would create a `33.33 ms` visible interval about every `16.68 s` and would require a fresh latency budget.
6. **Measure content presentation.** Distinguish a scheduled hold, a missing midpoint, a capture drop, and a skipped drawable. Count intervals between actual presentations of successive content IDs during moving gameplay. A fresh drawable containing the old image cannot turn a missed midpoint into a successful interval. Report startup, steady-state and cadence breaks separately, alongside the full-run total.

## 7. What I would cut from the first version

- **Advanced scaling.** Ship the shared bilinear path through the acceptance tests. The first comparison would be MetalFX spatial at the exact output size. `MTLFXSpatialScaler.h` declares `MTLFXSpatialScalerDescriptor.supportsDevice:`, `outputWidth`, `outputHeight`, `newSpatialScalerWithDevice:`, and `MTLFXSpatialScaler.encodeToCommandBuffer:`. For a later VT comparison, query the exact size using `VTLowLatencySuperResolutionScalerConfiguration.supportedScaleFactorsForFrameWidth:frameHeight:` in `VTFrameProcessor_LowLatencySuperResolutionScaler.h`.
- **Combined VT interpolation/upscaling, MetalFX frame generation, alternate models and HDR.** **M, reported in `OPTIONS.md`:** temporal-only maximum dimension `1920`, pixel cap `2,073,600`, combined-upscale maximum input dimension `640`. The applicable query symbols are `maximumDimensionForSpatialScaleFactor:` and `maximumPixelCountForSpatialScaleFactor:` in `VTFrameProcessor_LowLatencyFrameInterpolation.h`. `MTLFXFrameInterpolator.h` exposes `MTLFXFrameInterpolatorBase.depthTexture` and `motionTexture`; the stated HDMI capture provides neither game texture. [Project measurements](~/Developer/ps5-framegen/OPTIONS.md)
- **An OBS relay, webcam-specific live tuning, and elaborate HUD graphics.** Preserve the counters, content IDs, interval graph, latency estimate, audio calibration and capture-only toggle. Those are needed to decide whether the app works.

## 8. Open questions that need a measurement

- What are the fastest capture-only and generated-mode TV-relative photon differentials for the **same real events**? Report median, p95, p99, worst observed, and camera uncertainty. The project gives latency ceilings without specifying a percentile, so report excursions as well as typical results.
- Does the Elgato expose the requested uncompressed mode while the existing OBS stream is active? Do repeated input frames remain identical across both active planes? How much time does a required conversion or IOSurface copy add?
- Does the panel sustain the requested source-matched refresh throughout fullscreen playback? How often do actual content intervals miss the target window, and how many rolling target-length glitch windows fail?
- Does idle VT pass its p99 gate? Under paced load, what fraction of pairs miss the **whole** midpoint deadline, and which stage used the margin?
- How many synthetic frames survive a cadence break incorrectly, and how often do HUD motion, flashes, or noisy repeats cause false decisions? Does the chosen scaler preserve acceptable detail within the same deadline?
- What audio offset and jitter remain on the intended output device in both modes and across a mode switch?

## Not verified

- Actual PS5 title mode, live source rate, HDMI duplicate phase, SDR/color metadata, and behavior in menus, static scenes, or alternate game modes. The supplied hardware and signal specifications were used as planning inputs.
- Elgato native formats, transport encoding, timestamp provenance within the card, callback jitter, buffer compatibility, zero-copy operation, hardware latency, and simultaneous OBS capture.
- Idle VT performance, paced sustained performance, deadline-miss rate, thermal behavior, and the minimum VT call duration. The verified log covers the supplied loaded run only. The machine capability limits are project-reported measurements in `OPTIONS.md`; I did not repeat the capability probe.
- Exact refresh rates the panel will grant, slot prediction accuracy, compositor buffering, render lead, scanout and pixel response, and whether the source-matched schedule passes the glitch requirement. The timing and drift figures are conditional arithmetic.
- Every engineering allocation and heuristic proposed here, including ring capacities, detector timing windows, cut threshold, display-period filter, audio handling, and the adequacy of four display slots on real hardware.
- Live duplicate equality, recording-specific decision thresholds, cadence recovery quality, interpolation artifacts, scaler quality and cost, audio-device latency, and attainable A/V sync. No independent comparison with optical flow or RIFE was performed.
- End-to-end acceptance on all targets. I read the project evidence and SDK headers, made no file changes, and ran no build or new benchmark.
